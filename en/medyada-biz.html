UZUNGÖL, A PARADISE DISCOVERED 30 YEARS AGO

One of Trabzon's and the region's major tourism destinations, Uzungöl soon overcame the difficulties of being discovered late. Formed when a landslide blocked the Solaklı stream in 1760, Uzungöl is adorned with pine trees like a bride. Tourism was limited, and the region relied on hazelnuts, agriculture and livestock until the İnan family introduced tourism to Uzungöl. In 1975, the family began serving highland visitors with a small 12-square-metre room containing two tables and a fish pond they had built. Their love of hospitality never diminished over the following 30 years. Seeing the opportunity presented by highlanders travelling to the plateaus between May and October, the İnan brothers gradually built bungalows for them to stay in. At the end of the 1980s, they raised their ambitions to make the region a centre of attraction. Although local people did not believe in their plans, they continued investing with all their strength. Constantly reinvesting their earnings, the İnan brothers now own some of the region's most important tourism facilities. Meanwhile, some of those who did not believe in the family at the time now run businesses in Uzungöl themselves.

Wood instead of concrete

With support from the media, Uzungöl came to the attention of Turkey and the world in the early 1990s. The municipality changed its zoning plan to favour wooden buildings instead of permitting concrete developments. As the region became more attractive, operators pursuing new projects even drew attention with wooden fire escapes. More than 20 businesses are now working to serve tourism in the region. With demand particularly high in June, July, August and September, operators facing a shortage of beds also turn their own homes into guesthouses. Under local decisions, nobody can even hammer in a nail without municipal permission. An intensive programme to improve the area has been underway since this year, including moving telephone and electricity cables underground, creating walking routes, and widening and improving the central road.

An influx of tourists

Uzungöl welcomes many visitors, particularly from Arab countries, as well as Israel, the USA, Japan, Australia, the United Kingdom, Italy, Germany, Russia, Canada, Korea, China, Japan, Spain, France and other countries. Its accommodation facilities have a capacity of 850 guests. Many figures from Turkey's arts and political circles have also visited Uzungöl. These include Prime Minister Recep Tayyip Erdoğan, Recai Kutan, İsmet İnönü, Hüseyin Kıvrıkoğlu, Doğan Güreş, Fikri Sağlar, foreign ambassadors, civil servants and businesspeople.

Special surroundings

The thousands of tourists who visit Uzungöl each year can enjoy a region where green and blue come together. Those tired of noise and crowds can enjoy a quiet, peaceful holiday here. Visitors also have the opportunity to explore attractions such as the Zigana Mountain facilities, with bungalows, local food, skiing and walking routes, as well as Sümela Monastery, Hagia Sophia Church and Sera Lake.

A festival, too

In response to growing tourist interest in recent years, local authorities and civil organisations in Uzungöl began arranging a festival in August. Held over the last two years, the festival has brought domestic and international visitors together around the lake, holding hands and enjoying hours of entertainment. Alongside pedal-boat trips on the lake, vehicle safaris are organised to the highlands and mountains. Each facility prepares to provide live music according to guest demand. The area has truly become a tourism centre. Visitors make plans to return, while operators develop projects for further investment.

How to get there?

Uzungöl is 90 kilometres from Trabzon Airport. From the airport, head towards Rize and turn towards Çaykara at the entrance to Of. Continue inland through the Solaklı Valley, admiring its magnificent natural scenery and a green landscape so rich that it can overwhelm first-time visitors. Along the route are shops selling tourist goods and souvenirs. The road problems the region once experienced have been resolved. Except for two stretches of 100 metres each, the entire road to Uzungöl is paved. At the end of the climb, mosque minarets announce your arrival. After reaching the town centre, follow the lakeside road to the facilities with bungalows and wooden buildings. Uzungöl enchants visitors with its clean air and magnificent pine trees. Even in August, bring a few sweaters and a jacket along with your summer clothes: the high altitude can make evening walks cold. For those without a car, vehicles leave Trabzon's central Çömlekçi district for the area every hour. In summer, it is not possible to find accommodation without booking ahead. All facilities have parking in front for visitors arriving by car.

What to eat?

No visit to Uzungöl is complete without tasting the local trout. Trout fried in butter is particularly appetising. It is also served in casseroles, on a griddle or grilled. Beyond trout, you can find local kuymak, cabbage soup, stuffed cabbage leaves, lamb and beef, rice pudding, strained yoghurt, chicken and other meat dishes. There is no doubt that you will find delicious food to suit your palate. Vakfıkebir bread should not be forgotten either: it is available in almost every establishment in the region. From soups and ayran to salads, there are flavours for everyone.

Uzungöl Has a Different Beauty in Every Season

Located 99 kilometres from Trabzon and 19 kilometres from Çaykara, at an altitude of 1,090 metres, Uzungöl surpasses the beauty of the Alps with its steep slopes and magnificent forests. Located 99 kilometres from Trabzon and 19 kilometres from Çaykara, at an altitude of 1,090 metres, Uzungöl surpasses the beauty of the Alps with its steep slopes and magnificent forests. Uzungöl takes its name from the lake formed when a landslide blocked the stream bed in the Haldizen Valley. Its natural beauty, fresh air, traditional wooden houses and the lake that completes the setting draw visitors in. Attracting great interest from domestic and international travellers, Uzungöl has considerable tourism potential. Activities include trekking, birdwatching and botanical excursions, along with trips to lakes among the higher mountains and to other highland pastures. Bears, wolves, wild goats and grouse live in the mountains around Uzungöl. The area takes on a different beauty each season, and winter tourism activities have also developed in recent years. Although still uncommon, snowmobile trips and helicopter skiing are attracting increasing numbers of visitors from Turkey and abroad. With its surrounding spruce forests and high peaks, Uzungöl resembles a natural landscape exhibition and is well worth exploring for nature lovers. To reach Uzungöl, first travel to Trabzon by air or road. Direct transport is available to Uzungöl, 99 kilometres from the city centre. Visitors arriving from Rize or Artvin can turn onto the Çaykara road at Of. Uzungöl has the infrastructure needed to meet basic needs, with hotels, motels and guesthouses for accommodation. Those who enjoy living close to nature can also stay in a tent.
